[Page 9 — Results]

Now I will present our results and next steps.

We evaluated both models on the same 109 studies from 55 patients. The seen model included physician A's cases during training, while the unseen model excluded them.

Their A-U-R-O-C scores were very close: zero point six seven three for seen, and zero point six seven seven for unseen. This measures how well the model ranks positive cases above negative ones; it is not accuracy.

The unseen model also had slightly better precision-recall and Brier scores. Both models performed better than the constant baseline on these measures, although we did not separately test whether that improvement was statistically significant.

[Page 10 — Uncertainty]

However, these small differences do not establish a clear advantage.

We used one thousand paired bootstrap samples, resampling patients. All three confidence intervals included zero. Therefore, this pilot provides no clear evidence of a performance difference between the two training conditions.

This does not prove that the models are equivalent. The intervals also capture uncertainty from test patients, not from retraining with different random seeds.

[Page 11 — Conclusion and next steps]

Our main achievement is a working comparison using real images.

The main limitations are one physician group, one training seed, small samples, and labels derived from reports rather than independent image review.

Next, we plan to include more physicians and seeds, use the larger prepared training sets, check labels marked as not mentioned, and explore case mix and higher image resolution.

Finally, this study does not judge physician quality or demonstrate a causal physician effect.

Thank you.
